About UNZANASA

Discover the history, governance, and people behind the University of Zambia Natural Sciences Student Association.

Est. ~1970s

Our Story

UNZANASA traces its roots to the founding of the University of Zambia School of Natural Sciences, where pioneering natural sciences students recognised the need for a structured body to represent their academic, welfare, and professional interests.

Over over 50 years, UNZANASA has grown from a small student committee into a nationally recognised association with over 800 active members, international affiliations through Science Networks, and a proud legacy of community service, sporting excellence, and academic achievement.

Today, UNZANASA continues to be the voice of every natural sciences student at UNZA — advocating for better training conditions, organising life-changing outreach programmes, and producing scientists who lead Zambia's research and innovation sector.

Historical Milestones

1966

University of Zambia Founded

The University of Zambia was officially established on 22 July 1966 at Great East Road, Lusaka — just two years after Zambia's independence.

Early 1970s

School of Natural Sciences Established

UNZA's School of Natural Sciences was established, beginning the training of Zambia's first locally-educated natural scientists. Science students immediately formed informal academic networks.

Mid 1970s

UNZANASA Formally Founded

Science students formalised their association, adopting a constitution and electing the first Executive Committee. UNZANASA became the official body representing all UNZA natural sciences students.

1980s

Community Outreach Programmes Begin

UNZANASA launched its first structured rural health outreach programmes, sending student teams to underserved communities across Zambia — a tradition that continues today.

1990s

Science Network Membership Achieved

UNZANASA became a full member of the International Federation of Natural Sciences Students' Associations (Science Networks), connecting Zambian students to a global network spanning 130+ countries.

2000s

Annual Natural Sciences Conference Established

The UNZANASA Annual Natural Sciences Conference was launched as a flagship event, providing a platform for student research presentations, keynote speakers, and professional development.

2010s

Sports & Cultural Programme Expansion

UNZANASA formalised its Sports Section, fielding teams in football, basketball, volleyball, athletics and more — competing in inter-faculty and inter-university tournaments.

2020s

Digital Transformation & Research Growth

UNZANASA embraced digital platforms for member management, research mentorship, and outreach coordination. Student research publications increased significantly, with several appearing in peer-reviewed African health journals.

2026

UNZANASA Today

With over 800 active members, 10+ institutional partners, a thriving sports culture, and a growing research programme, UNZANASA continues to shape the future of science in Zambia.

Governance

Constitution & Governance

UNZANASA is governed by its official Constitution, first enacted in 2003 and last amended in 2022 by the General Assembly. The document defines the identity, membership, governance structures, powers, and procedures of the association.

📜

UNZANASA Constitution — 2022 Amended Edition

First enacted 2003 · Amended 2016 & 2022 · Adopted by the General Assembly, University of Zambia

Preamble
In the Name of Scientific Excellence for All

We, the natural sciences students of the University of Zambia, recognising the need for a united, dynamic and forward-looking association, do hereby establish the University of Zambia Natural Sciences Student Association (UNZANASA).

We are committed to fostering academic excellence, research culture, student welfare, community service and international cooperation among Zambia's future scientists. This Constitution is the supreme governing document of the association and shall bind all members, officers and committees.

Part I — Identity
Name, Colours, Motto & Seat
  • Full Name: University of Zambia Natural Sciences Student Association
  • Abbreviation: UNZANASA
  • Colours: Green, Red and Gold
  • Motto: "Strength Through Wisdom"
  • Seat: University of Zambia, School of Natural Sciences, Lusaka, Zambia
  • Year Founded: 1966 (constitutionally formalised 2003)
Part II — Aims & Objectives
What UNZANASA Exists to Do
  • To promote and safeguard the academic, professional and social interests of natural sciences students at the University of Zambia.
  • To foster a spirit of unity, cooperation and solidarity among members of the association.
  • To promote excellence in scientific education and research.
  • To represent natural sciences students in academic, institutional and national forums.
  • To engage in community health outreach and promote the health and welfare of Zambian communities.
  • To liaise and cooperate with the International Federation of Natural Sciences Students' Associations (Science Networks) and other regional bodies.
  • To promote cultural, social and sporting activities among members.
  • To create platforms for members to develop leadership, communication and professional skills.
Part III — Membership
Who Belongs to UNZANASA

Membership of UNZANASA shall be open to all students registered in the BSc Natural Sciences degree programme at the University of Zambia School of Natural Sciences. There are two categories:

  • Full Members: All currently registered BSc Natural Sciences students who have paid their annual membership subscription. Full members have the right to vote, hold office and access all association benefits.
  • Associate Members: Postgraduate students and alumni of the School of Natural Sciences who wish to remain affiliated with the association. Associate members do not have voting rights or eligibility to hold executive office.

Membership Fee: Determined by the Executive Committee and ratified by the General Assembly each academic year. Fees are used exclusively for association activities and development.

Part IV — Governance Organs
Structure of Authority

UNZANASA is governed through three organs, listed in order of authority:

🏛️
General Assembly

The supreme authority. Comprises all full members. Meets at least once per semester. Approves the budget, elects the Executive Committee, ratifies constitutional amendments, and considers matters of fundamental policy.

👥
Executive Committee

Elected annually by the General Assembly. Manages the day-to-day affairs of the association, implements General Assembly decisions, and coordinates all standing committees.

📋
Standing Committees

Specialist sub-committees that execute the work of specific portfolios. Each is chaired by the relevant executive portfolio holder and reports to the full Executive Committee.

Executive Committee — Roles & Responsibilities

Position Portfolio Key Responsibilities (per Constitution)
President Leadership Chief executive officer of UNZANASA. Chairs the Executive Committee and General Assembly. Represents the association to the university, government and international bodies. Signs official documents.
Vice-President Internal Affairs Deputises for the President. Coordinates internal association programmes, liaises with the university administration, oversees committee work, and assists in day-to-day management.
Secretary General Administration Maintains records of all meetings (minutes), handles official correspondence, manages the association's membership register, and coordinates constitutional compliance.
Treasurer Finance Manages all association finances, collects membership fees, maintains financial records, prepares budgets and financial reports for General Assembly approval, and ensures financial transparency.
Academic Affairs Secretary Academics Chairs the Academic Committee. Organises tutorial programmes, manages the past paper repository (organised by class, course and year), coordinates laboratory skills workshops, and promotes academic welfare.
Secretary of Research Research Chairs the Research Committee. Promotes student research culture, coordinates mentorship programmes, facilitates publications and grant applications, and liaises with faculty researchers.
Social Welfare Officer Welfare Addresses the physical, mental and social wellbeing of members. Manages welfare funds, emergency support systems, and mental health awareness initiatives.
Sports Secretary Sports & Recreation Organises all association sports activities, manages teams, coordinates inter-faculty tournaments and the annual UNZANASA Sports Day.
Public Relations Officer Media & Communications Manages all external and internal communications, social media platforms, newsletter, photography, and branding of the association.
Community Service Secretary Outreach Plans and executes health outreach camps, health education campaigns, and coordinates volunteer activities in Zambian communities.
Science Networks Officer International Affairs Manages UNZANASA's membership with Science Networks. Coordinates international exchange programmes, global health advocacy, and Science Networks General Assembly attendance.
Part V — Elections
How the Executive Committee is Elected
  • Elections are held annually at the end of each academic year.
  • All candidates must be full members in good standing and must be registered BSc Natural Sciences students.
  • Nominations are submitted in writing to the Secretary General at least two weeks before the election date.
  • Voting is by secret ballot of full members present at a duly convened General Assembly.
  • A candidate must obtain a simple majority (50%+1) of votes cast.
  • In the event of a tie, a run-off vote is held at the same meeting.
  • No person may hold the same executive position for more than two consecutive terms.
  • An Interim Executive may be appointed by the General Assembly if vacancies occur mid-term.
Part VI — Constitutional Amendments
How This Constitution May be Changed
  • Any proposal to amend this Constitution must be submitted in writing to the Secretary General at least 30 days before a General Assembly.
  • Amendments require a two-thirds (⅔) majority of full members present and voting at the General Assembly.
  • No amendment may be made to alter the fundamental aims and objectives of the association without the consent of three-quarters (¾) of the full membership.
  • All amendments must be recorded in the official minutes and incorporated in a revised edition of the Constitution within 60 days.

This Constitution was first enacted in 2003, amended in 2016, and most recently revised in 2022 by the UNZANASA General Assembly.

Why does benzene not undergo addition reactions despite having double bonds?
I know it's related to resonance but I can't fully explain it in an exam — what's the key argument?
Organic ChemAromaticity
✅ Best Answer · T. Banda (Year 3) · 1 day ago
The 6 π electrons in benzene are fully delocalised over all six carbons (aromatic system). The resonance stabilisation (~150 kJ/mol) means addition would destroy the ring and produce a less stable product. Benzene therefore strongly prefers substitution, which keeps the ring intact. For the exam: mention (1) delocalisation, (2) resonance/aromaticity, (3) preference for substitution over addition.

Why does the ethanol emulsion test only turn cloudy when water is added — not when ethanol is first added?
I put this in my discussion but the marker said it was wrong. The oil seemed clear in ethanol. Why?
BIO1400Lipids
✅ Best Answer · Academic Affairs Sec · 4 days ago
Oil dissolves in ethanol because both are non-polar — "like dissolves like" — so the mixture stays clear. When you add water (polar), the oil can no longer stay dissolved and gets dispersed as tiny micelles (hydrophobic ends pointing inward). These micelles scatter light, producing the cloudy white emulsion. So: ethanol step = dissolve the lipid; water step = force it to precipitate as micelles. Both steps are needed.

Firebase Configuration Status

⚠ Firebase not yet configured. The site is live but the admin dashboard needs your Firebase project credentials to store data in the cloud. Follow the steps below.
  1. Go to console.firebase.google.com → your unzanasa project
  2. Click the web app icon </> → copy the firebaseConfig object
  3. Open index.html in a text editor
  4. Find the block that says PASTE_YOUR_API_KEY_HERE (near line 930)
  5. Replace all PASTE_YOUR_…_HERE values with your real config values
  6. Save the file → drag it back onto netlify.com/drop (or go to your Netlify site → Deploys → drag & drop)
  7. The dashboard will then use real Firebase Auth and Firestore — all papers, news, events and polls will be stored in the cloud
const firebaseConfig = {
  apiKey: "YOUR_API_KEY",
  authDomain: "unzanasa.firebaseapp.com",
  projectId: "unzanasa",
  storageBucket: "unzanasa.appspot.com",
  messagingSenderId: "YOUR_SENDER_ID",
  appId: "YOUR_APP_ID"
};

Once configured and redeployed, create executive accounts in Firebase Console → Authentication → Users → Add user. Then add a matching document in Firestore → executives collection → document ID = the user's UID → fields: name, role.
